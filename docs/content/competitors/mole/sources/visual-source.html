    Clean up your Mac

    Review caches and build output before reclaiming space. Personal data stays out of the list, Clean leaves local AI models alone, and AI sessions past the window you choose and Ollama models unused for 30 days are removed only from AI Cleanup & Care, after you confirm.

    See the list before reclaiming space

    Mole scans app and system caches, logs, temporary files, developer tools, browsers, cloud clients, and AI coding tools. Each item is listed with its name, path, and size. Easily recreated caches come first; files that take time to download or rebuild are left for you to review.

    Sort data by what it costs to get back

    The product grew out of a practical rule described in the story of building Mole for Mac. Every candidate falls into one of three groups:

    1. Regenerable: HTTP and GPU caches, build output, superseded tool versions, and most logs can return automatically.
    2. Expensive to rebuild: package downloads, device support, and local model weights cost time or bandwidth, so Mole does not treat them as routine cleanup.
    3. Irreplaceable: documents, mail, photos, project state, and conversation history never belong in a one-click cleaning list.

    Clean starts with app caches, which include browser and cloud-client caches and app logs; developer, AI, communication and design tools have their own groups. Uninstalled-app leftovers follow, then System Data: local Time Machine snapshots, system caches and logs, old macOS installers and stale wallpaper downloads. Trash comes last. The Manage Separately group lists backups, local iCloud files, virtual machines and models, outside the cleanup total.

    A separate action under Manage Separately can reclaim unused space inside a supported Parallels virtual disk through Parallels’ own tool. Shut down the VM normally first; suspended or running VMs are not eligible. Review the estimate and confirm separately. The action keeps the VM and its files, does not join bulk cleanup, and must finish without interruption.

    Clean AI build debris, protect AI memory

    Coding agents can produce many rounds of target, dist, .next, and DerivedData in a day. One real cleanup recovered 86 GB of build output. Mole can surface that regenerable weight while Clean leaves Ollama and Hugging Face model stores alone, along with Claude, Codex, and Grok session histories, memories, plans, skills, and generated images; idle Ollama models are removed only on the AI page when you pick them. Models remain visible in Analyze so their owners can manage them correctly.

    Old sessions are a different decision, so they get a page of their own. When this Mac holds AI tool data, a far Moon appears on the Clean page and leads to AI Cleanup & Care: Codex sessions past a 15 to 120 day window you choose, Claude Code sessions past their window whose project folder is gone, cleanable worktrees, superseded CLI builds, and command-line tools unused for 30 days. Caches and old versions start checked, sessions and tools wait for you, and any single Codex session can be unchecked. A Codex session is copied to the Trash before Codex's own command removes it, so its index stays consistent. The test of nearly 30 AI tools shows what each one leaves behind.

    Retention settings apply separately to Codex, Claude Code, OpenCode and Devin: 15, 30, 90 or 120 days, or Don't list, the default, so no session is listed until you pick a window; until then each row shows how many sessions the tool keeps and their size, and each choice shows what it would list. Sessions used in the last 24 hours stay out, and worktrees with unique changes or commits are retained. Ollama models unused for a month are a separate, unchecked choice removed through ollama rm; running models and libraries whose ownership cannot be confirmed are withheld. Other model stores stay out of this action.

    Running apps and protected paths stay out

    Caches owned by an open app are skipped. Every removal is checked again against protected paths at execution time, and anything outside the current operation is refused. Full Disk Access expands what Mole can measure under Library; it never disables these checks. File names, contents, and scan results stay on the Mac.

    Choose how to remove caches

    Caches are permanently deleted by default to free up space. You can change this in Settings to move them to Trash and keep the option to restore them. Ordinary file removal in the other tools also uses Trash, and completed actions are recorded in the local operations log.

    Clean when you need to

    Mole does not send scheduled cleanup reminders or keep scanning your disk in the background. Open Clean when you need space, review the list, and choose what to remove. Scanning is free, and you can complete two cleanups before purchasing. Download Mole or read which files a Mac cleaner should leave alone.
